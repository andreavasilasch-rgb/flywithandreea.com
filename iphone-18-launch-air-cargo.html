Air cargo

One launch day. Every country at once.

A phone crosses six continents before it is switched on. Here is the aviation behind an iPhone 18 or foldable iPhone launch: the components, the materials, the colours, and the freighters that put every box on the shelf the same morning.

Every country. The same morning.

Somewhere right now a pallet is being weighed, a customs file is being pre-cleared and a widebody freighter is being loaded nose first, so that a box can be opened in Sydney, Dubai, London and Los Angeles on the same day, at the same hour, as though distance had been switched off.

That is the part of a phone launch nobody films. It is also, if you love aircraft, the best part. Because a modern phone is an aviation product long before it is a consumer one. It flies as raw material, it flies as component, it flies as finished goods, and it flies again as the thing you queued for.

A phone crosses more borders before it is switched on than most people cross in a lifetime.

Did you know

A smartphone can contain up to 75 of the 118 elements on the periodic table. More than 200 companies supply the parts, drawn from suppliers in around 43 countries across six continents. Almost every one of those hand-offs involves a flight.

What is actually in the box.

Open a phone and you are looking at a small museum of the planet. Aluminium for the frame, pressed and machined from bauxite that began in Australia or Guinea. Copper for the coils and the antenna lines, most often from Chile. Gold on the contacts, because gold does not corrode and a contact that corrodes is a phone that fails. Silver in the solder paste. Lithium and cobalt in the cell. Rare earth elements in the speakers and in the little motor that makes the phone buzz in your pocket.

Then the glass. Chemically strengthened, ion exchanged, polished flat to a tolerance you cannot see. Then the display, the image sensors, the modem, the memory, the processor. Each one made somewhere different, each one measured, each one time critical, each one small enough and valuable enough that the only sensible way to move it is by air.

Did you know

Colour is chemistry, not paint. Anodising grows a hard oxide layer on the aluminium and the dye goes into the pores of that layer, which is why a foldable iPhone hinge or an iPhone 18 frame keeps its shade after a year in a pocket. It also means colour is decided at the factory, months ahead, and a late change of mix is a logistics problem before it is a marketing one.

This is why launch colours leak through the supply chain rather than through the press office. A new shade means a new anodising run, new packaging artwork, new pallet counts and a different split across the freighters. The aircraft plan changes because the dye changed.

Why a phone flies.

Air freight is priced on the greater of actual weight and volumetric weight, so the goods that fly happily are the ones with a lot of value packed into very little space. A phone is close to the perfect air cargo: light, small, expensive, fragile in the ways that air handling protects, and worthless the moment it is late.

That last point is the whole argument. A launch has a date. The date is announced, the adverts are bought, the stores are staffed, the reviews are embargoed to the hour. Sea freight takes weeks and cannot be rescheduled. A freighter can be booked, loaded and airborne the same night.

Did you know

Electronics are what the freight world calls low density cargo. They fill an aircraft by volume long before they fill it by weight, which is why launch season is measured in cubic metres and pallet positions rather than tonnes, and why the very large freighters earn their keep even when the load looks light on paper.

The aircraft that do the work.

There is no single cargo aeroplane, in the same way there is no single van. There is a right size for the job, and the skill is choosing it. Here is the range, from the aircraft that lands on a short island strip to the aircraft that swallows a production line.

Did you know

The 747 freighter nose door exists because of length, not weight. Hinging the whole nose upward lets a load slide straight down the centreline of the main deck, which is how aircraft engines, rotor blades and complete production tooling travel. It is one of the few design features in aviation that is genuinely irreplaceable.

Choosing between these is not about prestige. It is about pallet positions, door dimensions, runway length, the field elevation on the day, the temperature at the airport at the hour you want to depart, and whether the cargo can be split. Get it right and the load leaves tonight. Get it wrong and it waits.

The same day, everywhere.

Here is the choreography that makes a worldwide launch possible, and it is more elegant than most people imagine.

Production finishes in waves, not all at once. The first wave goes to the markets that are furthest away in flying hours, because they need the head start. Later waves go to the markets closest to the factory. The goal is not to ship everything at the same time. The goal is for everything to arrive at the same time, which is the opposite problem and a much more interesting one.

Time zones do the rest of the work. A freighter leaving East Asia in the evening lands in Europe in the morning of the same calendar day. Cross the Pacific eastbound and you can land before you took off. Launch planners use that. A single aircraft can serve two continents in a rotation if the ground time is short enough and the customs file is already sitting with the authority before the wheels touch, which is where a good freight forwarder earns their fee.

Did you know

Most launch stock clears customs while it is still in the air. The paperwork is lodged in advance, the goods sit in a bonded facility on arrival, and duty is paid when they leave for the stores. The aircraft is rarely the slow part. Paperwork prepared late is the slow part, and that is the one variable a good charter plan removes.

Getting cargo into the air is the easy half. Getting it out of the airport is where a shipment is won or lost.

It is not only phones. Look in your kitchen.

Launch logistics are not unique to the phone in your hand. The same aircraft, the same pallets and the same overnight thinking move a surprising amount of what you own.

A small appliance is a volumetric puzzle. An Instant Pot Duo pressure cooker, an Instant Pot Duo Plus 9-in-1 electric pressure cooker, an Instant Pot Duo Crisp or a Ninja Twisti high speed blender duo is mostly air inside a box. It weighs little and occupies a lot, so it is priced on volume and it rewards clever pallet building rather than brute strength. Seasonal peaks around gifting weeks are planned months ahead, and the late top up flights are what keep shelves full in the last week.

Cosmetics fly under stricter rules and it is a genuinely fascinating corner of the business. Anything alcohol based or pressurised is regulated cargo, packed, labelled and documented to a defined standard, with trained handlers at both ends. A shipment of an Estee Lauder Double Wear custom coverage correcting duo or a Maybelline Master Contour V Shape duo stick is a compliance exercise as much as a freight one, in the same family as pharmaceutical shipments, and the operators who do it well are very good indeed.

Then there is the aftermarket. Restored Apple iPhone 15 Pro, restored Apple iPhone 14 Pro Max, restored Apple iPhone 16 Pro, restored Apple iPhone 13 and similar refurbished stock travels in the opposite direction to new product, gathering into regional hubs for grading and repair, then flying back out to the markets that want them. It is a real and growing air freight flow, and it is quietly one of the most sustainable things in the industry.

Did you know

Accessories often travel separately from the device. AirPods, AirPods 5, iPhone duo cases, chargers and cables are cheaper per kilo and less time critical, so they can go earlier and by a slower route, while the devices themselves take the fast lane. One product family, two completely different transport plans.

The aviation questions, answered.

How does a new phone reach every country on the same day?

Production finishes in waves, and the waves are sequenced by flying time rather than by geography. The markets that are furthest away ship first, the nearest ship last, and the aim is a common arrival rather than a common departure. Customs files are lodged while the freight is still in the air, the goods rest in bonded facilities on arrival, and they move to stores once duty is settled.

Why do phones travel by air rather than by sea?

Because a launch has a fixed date and a phone has very high value in very little space. Sea freight takes weeks and cannot be re-timed. Air freight can be booked, loaded and airborne the same night, and for goods this valuable the freight cost is a small fraction of the retail value.

How many components are in a modern smartphone?

Hundreds of individual parts, supplied by more than 200 companies, drawn from suppliers in around 43 countries across six continents. The materials involved can span up to 75 of the 118 elements on the periodic table.

Which aircraft carries the most cargo?

Among production freighters the Boeing 747-8 freighter carries about 137.7 tonnes and opens at the nose. The Antonov An-124 carries 120 tonnes, and up to 150 tonnes on later variants, with a 750 cubic metre hold and ramps at both ends, which makes it the choice for outsize loads that will not fit anywhere else.

What is the smallest aircraft used for air cargo?

Single engine turboprops such as the Cessna 208B Grand Caravan, carrying about 1.4 tonnes into short strips and islands. They are the last leg of many supply chains, and often the only way to reach the final destination.

What is volumetric weight and why does it matter?

Air freight is charged on the greater of actual weight and volumetric weight, which is calculated from the dimensions of the shipment. Light and bulky goods such as small kitchen appliances are priced on volume, which is why pallet building is a genuine skill and why an aircraft can be full long before it is heavy.

Can lithium batteries be flown?

Yes, under defined rules. Lithium cells are regulated cargo with requirements covering state of charge, packaging, labelling, documentation and crew notification. It is routine work for operators who are approved for it, and the paperwork is prepared before the shipment moves.

How quickly can a cargo charter depart?

Often within hours for a suitable aircraft that is already in position, and the same day in most regions. The limiting factors are usually overflight and landing permits, airport slots and crew duty time rather than the aircraft itself, which is why the request should start the clock immediately.

What does a cargo charter cost?

It depends on aircraft type, sector length, positioning, handling and permits. You will be quoted one firm all in figure rather than a range, in your currency, within 30 minutes of sending the dimensions, the weight, the two airports and the deadline.

What is an AOG flight?

Aircraft on ground. A component failure has grounded an aircraft and a replacement part must reach it immediately. A small jet carrying one box is routinely cheaper than leaving a large aircraft parked, and these flights are among the most time critical in the industry.

Why are launch colours a logistics question?

Colour on an aluminium frame is anodised rather than painted, so the shade is fixed during manufacture. A change of colour mix means a change of production run, packaging, pallet counts and the split of stock across aircraft, which is why the flying plan is rebuilt whenever the colour plan changes.

Do accessories fly with the devices?

Usually not. Cases, chargers, cables and headphones are lower value per kilo and less time critical, so they can travel earlier or on a slower routing, while the devices take the fast lane. One product family can have several completely different transport plans.

Can cosmetics and beauty products be air freighted?

Yes. Alcohol based and pressurised products are regulated cargo and are shipped under the appropriate class, with correct packing, labelling, documentation and trained handling at both ends. It is a well established flow and one that rewards choosing an operator with the right approvals.

Do refurbished phones travel by air as well?

They do, and in the opposite direction. Refurbished stock gathers into regional hubs for grading and repair, then flies back out to the markets that want it. It is a real and growing freight flow, and it keeps a great many devices in use for years longer.

How do I start a cargo enquiry?

Send four things: the dimensions of the largest single piece, the total weight, the two airports, and the time it has to be there. That is enough to select an aircraft and return a firm price. Anything else can follow.

What is chargeable weight?

The figure your price is calculated on. It is the greater of the actual weight and the volumetric weight, and volumetric weight comes from the dimensions of the shipment. Light and bulky freight is charged on the space it occupies, which is why the shape of a pallet matters as much as the scale reading.

Can I charter a freighter one way?

Yes, and most cargo charters are one way. The aircraft has to reach you and it has to go somewhere afterwards, so positioning is part of the price. Where an aircraft is already close to your departure airport, or already due to fly your direction, the number improves noticeably.

What is a nose loading aircraft?

An aircraft whose nose hinges upward so freight can be loaded straight down the centreline of the main deck. It is how very long items travel, and the 747 freighter is the best known example. For loads that are long rather than heavy, it is often the only sensible answer.

Which aircraft would carry launch stock to 65 countries?

Not one aircraft but a rotation of them. Long range widebody freighters such as the 777 and 747 freighter handle the trunk routes, then narrowbody freighters and turboprops feed the smaller markets from regional hubs. The pattern is a hub and spoke network built for a single date.

How fragile is a folding screen in transit?

Treated properly, it travels perfectly well. Flexible displays are shipped flat, in anti static packaging, with the hinge protected and the stack cushioned against point loads. The care is in the packing and the handling, not in the flying, and air freight involves far less vibration than a long road leg.

What is a bonded warehouse?

A secure facility where imported goods sit before duty is paid. Launch stock frequently rests in bond on arrival, which lets it clear the airport quickly and wait close to the market until the release date. It is one of the reasons an aircraft can land days early without the product appearing early. The same facilities serve energy and automotive shipments all year round.

Can you fly aerosols and alcohol based cosmetics?

Yes, as regulated cargo, with the correct class, packing, labelling, documentation and trained handling at both ends. It is routine work for approved operators. The important part is telling us at the enquiry stage, because it changes which aircraft and which handler are suitable.

Can you get a pallet into a small airport at short notice?

Often yes. Turboprop freighters and light jets reach runways that a widebody cannot use, and they can be positioned quickly. The questions are runway length, the width of the cargo door, whether ground handling equipment is available, and the opening hours of the airport.

What information do you need to quote me?

Four things: the dimensions of the largest single piece, the total weight, the two airports, and the deadline. Add the nature of the goods if they are regulated. That is enough to select an aircraft and return a firm all in price.

How far ahead should I book launch season capacity?

As early as you can name a window. Late summer into autumn is the busiest period in the air cargo year, because consumer electronics, seasonal retail and gifting stock all compete for the same aircraft. Booking early is usually the difference between choosing an aircraft and taking whatever is left.

What is Duo on iPhone, and why is it not working?

This is a different Duo. Google Duo was a video calling app that was merged into Google Meet, so the standalone Duo app no longer exists. If your old Duo will not open or you are moving to a new phone, install Meet and sign in with the same account, and your contacts and history come across.

Who came first, Samsung or iPhone?

Samsung by a long way. The company dates to 1938 and was selling mobile phones from the late 1980s. The first iPhone arrived in 2007 and changed the shape of the market rather than starting it.

Did an iPhone 1 exist, and is it rare?

The original iPhone, often called the iPhone 2G, launched in 2007. Used examples are common and inexpensive. Sealed, factory original units are genuinely rare and have sold at auction for six figures, which says more about collectors than about telephones.

Is the iPhone 2G still usable today?

As a phone, not really. The 2G networks it relies on have been switched off in most countries and the apps and security updates ended long ago. As an object to own and admire, it is wonderful.

Why is Apple cheaper in Dubai?

Tax, mostly. The UAE applies 5% VAT, against 20% in the UK and higher combined duty and tax in several other markets. Add a strong retail presence and competitive currency and the shelf price lands lower. It is the same logic that makes Dubai to London one of the busiest private routes in the world. It is not a different phone, it is a different tax base.

Is the iPhone cheaper in India or the UAE?

The UAE is usually cheaper. India applies import duty and GST that push the local price up, which is why the same model can differ meaningfully between the two. Always compare the final price including tax, and remember that warranty terms are local.

Which country has the cheapest iPhone?

It moves with currency and tax, so any single answer dates quickly. Low tax markets such as the UAE, along with the United States where advertised prices exclude sales tax, tend to sit at the lower end. Japan is often competitive when the yen is weak.

What else is cheaper in Dubai than in the UK?

Broadly, goods where tax makes up a large share of the price: electronics, jewellery, gold, fragrance and cosmetics. The 5% VAT rate is the main reason. Groceries and rent are a different story, and prices move, so check on the day.

Which is the cheapest iPhone in the UAE?

The entry model of the current range, which sits well below the Pro and the foldable. Prices change with each launch and with each retailer, so use the official store price as your reference point rather than a marketplace listing.

Why did Apple stop including chargers?

Apple removed the power adapter from the box in 2020, citing the number of adapters already in circulation and the packaging and shipping savings that follow. Smaller boxes mean more units per pallet and per aircraft, which is a real reduction in freight.

Does MagSafe drain the battery faster?

Wireless charging is less efficient than a cable and some energy is lost as heat, so a full charge draws more from the wall. It does not damage the battery in normal use. If you want the coolest, fastest charge, use a cable.

What is the smallest iPhone available now?

Of the phones on sale, the entry e series has the most compact body. The Duo is an interesting case, because it folds down to a 5.4 inch outer screen and then opens to 7.6 inches, so it is both the smallest and the largest depending on the moment.

What is the secret button on the iPhone?

Most people mean Back Tap, in Accessibility settings under Touch. It turns a double or triple tap on the back of the phone into a shortcut, such as a screenshot, the torch or an app. It is genuinely useful and almost nobody knows it is there.

What will the 2027 iPhone be called?

Nobody outside Apple knows, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. Naming has moved around, with e, Air, Pro, Pro Max, Ultra and now Duo all in play, so the pattern is no longer a reliable predictor.

Which iPhone should I buy?

Honestly, the one that matches how you use it. Buy the Pro for the cameras and the display, the entry model if you mainly message and browse, and the Duo if a tablet sized screen in your pocket genuinely changes your day. All three are excellent, and the cheapest one that fits is usually the right one.

Should I turn my iPhone off at night?

There is no need. Modern iPhones manage charging and battery health on their own, including learning your routine and holding at 80% until you wake. An occasional restart is good housekeeping, but nightly shutdowns are not required.

Why does Gen Z prefer iPhone?

Mostly network effects. Messaging, media sharing and device handoff work smoothly within one ecosystem, and resale values hold up well. Design and camera quality matter too, but the social pull of everyone else already being there is the strongest single factor.
