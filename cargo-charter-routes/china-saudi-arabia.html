Hours against days

Why the flight time
is not the transit time

The aircraft is in the air for 7h 49m to 9h 04m. Nobody receives their cargo in 7h 49m to 9h 04m, and any page that tells you otherwise is quoting you one number for 2 different things.

The clock that matters to you starts at the warehouse cut off, not at departure. Miss it by 20 minutes and the consignment waits for the next service, which on some pairings is the next day. Then there is acceptance and build up at the origin, the flight itself, breakdown and handling at the far end, customs clearance at both ends, and the drive to the consignee.

So the honest answer has 2 parts. The flying is fixed and it is in the table above. Everything else is a question about cut off times, documents and handling, and that is where a day gets won or lost. Andreea quotes both, separately, and says which one is under her control.

Charter options

3 ways to buy
an air cargo charter

Before a rate means anything on this lane, it has to be clear which of the 3 purchases is being quoted. On China to Saudi Arabia the usual candidates are construction and project equipment and industrial machinery and spares, and the 2 are rarely bought the same way.

A full cargo aircraft charter is the whole deck for 1 customer. It is the expensive answer and sometimes the only 1, because a consignment that cannot be split cannot be flown any other way.

A part charter, or cargo air charter, takes a number of positions on a freighter already flying the sector. The saving against a full aircraft is large and the cost is that the departure is not yours to move.

Where a single piece is the problem, the purchase is a heavy air cargo charter. A heavy & outsize air cargo charter is sized by the door and the deck, which is why a 6 tonne piece can be simpler to fly than a 2 tonne 1 that happens to be 4 metres tall.

Asking for all 3 is the fastest way to find out whether an air cargo charter service understands the consignment, because the wrong answer is obvious the moment the aircraft is named.

Questions

What shippers
ask about this lane

How long does air cargo take from China to Saudi Arabia?

The flying is 7h 49m to 9h 04m depending on the airport pair, and the table on this page gives each one. Door to door is a different number and it is longer, because the clock includes the warehouse cut off at the origin, the handling, the customs at both ends and the delivery at the far end. Anyone quoting a single figure in days without saying what it is measured between is guessing.

What is the difference between flight time and transit time?

Flight time is wheels up to wheels down and it is arithmetic: the distance divided by the speed of the aircraft. Transit time is a commercial promise about the whole journey, and on a short lane the flying can be the smallest part of it. This page gives you the first honestly so you can judge the second.

How do you calculate air cargo charges?

In 2 steps. First the actual weight of the consignment in kilos, packaging and pallets included. Second the volumetric weight: length by width by height in centimetres, divided by 6,000, for every piece. Whichever of the 2 is larger is the chargeable weight, and that is the number the rate is applied to. The calculator on this page does both and tells you which one you are on.

What is the volumetric weight divisor for air freight?

6,000. Length by width by height in centimetres divided by 6,000 gives the volumetric weight in kilos, which is the same as saying 1 cubic metre counts as 167 kg. Sea freight and road freight use different divisors, so a figure worked out for a sea quote will not transfer to an air quote.

How much is air cargo per kilo?

There is no honest single figure, and a page that publishes one is publishing a number that was already out of date when it was typed. The rate per kilo on any lane moves with the season, the capacity flying that week, the service level and the handling the goods need. What does not move is the chargeable weight, which you can work out yourself above. Send that figure and Andreea returns one written all in price.

How much does air cargo cost from China to Saudi Arabia?

The price is the rate multiplied by the chargeable weight, plus the handling the consignment actually needs. The things that move it on this lane are the service level you buy, the season, whether the goods fit a standard pallet or have to be handled loose, and whether they need temperature control, security or a dangerous goods declaration. Work out the chargeable weight above, send the dimensions, and you get a written all in price rather than a range.

How much does it cost to charter a cargo plane?

It is priced by the aircraft and the rotation rather than by the kilo, so the drivers are the type, the distance, the positioning flights at either end, the crew hours, the fuel on the day, the ground handling and any permits the routing needs. That is why the same aircraft on the same lane is not the same price in 2 different weeks. A full charter earns its money when a consignment will not fit, cannot wait, or must not be split.

Is air cargo expensive compared with sea freight?

Per kilo, yes, by a wide margin. Per day of working capital tied up in stock, often not. Sea freight on a long lane is measured in weeks and air freight in days, so the comparison that matters is not the freight invoice on its own but the freight invoice against the cost of the goods sitting still. For high value, perishable or urgent cargo the arithmetic usually favours the aircraft.

Can you ship dangerous goods by air from China to Saudi Arabia?

Yes, under the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, with the classification, the packing group and the declaration agreed before anything is booked. Lithium batteries are the commonest case and the one most often declared wrongly, which is worth knowing before a consignment is turned away at acceptance.

Can you ship pharmaceuticals and perishables on this route?

Yes. The common bands are 2 to 8 C refrigerated, 15 to 25 C controlled room temperature and -20 C and below frozen. The band is set by the container rather than by the aircraft, and the handler at each end matters as much as the flight does, which is why the airport is chosen for the cargo rather than for the map.

Do I need a full aircraft, or can I buy part of one?

Usually part of one. Most of what moves on this lane goes as general cargo on a scheduled freighter. A part charter sits between that and a full aircraft and is the first thing worth asking about. The air cargo charter guide covers the difference.

Who handles the customs clearance?

Andreea coordinates the export clearance at China and the import clearance into Saudi Arabia, with the air waybill and any licence the goods themselves need. On air freight the paperwork fails more often than the aircraft does, which is why it is treated as part of the movement rather than as something you sort out separately.

Do you offer an air cargo charter service on China to Saudi Arabia, or only brokerage?

Both, and they are the same thing done properly. Andreea is a broker, which means she is not selling you her own aircraft and has no reason to recommend 1 over another. The air cargo charter service is the work around the flight: sourcing the aircraft, checking the consignment physically fits it, the permits, the handling at both ends and 1 person answering the phone while it is in the air.

What is the difference between a cargo air charter and a cargo aircraft charter?

A cargo aircraft charter is the whole aircraft for 1 customer. A cargo air charter, in the way most forwarders use it, is a block of positions on a freighter that is already operating. The first buys the schedule, the second buys the space. Where a single piece is the difficulty rather than the volume, the answer is usually neither of those but a heavy air cargo charter on an aircraft chosen for its door.

Can you charter an aircraft for livestock, horses or other live animals?

Yes, and it is 1 of the few kinds of freight where charter is often the sensible answer rather than the expensive one. A consignment of animals has to move in 1 go, at a time that suits the animals rather than a flight schedule, with handlers travelling alongside it. What decides the aircraft is the species, the number of head, the stalls or pens they travel in and the ground handling available at both ends. Tell Andreea those 4 things and the answer comes back as an aircraft type, not a rate.

What rules govern live animals by air?

The IATA Live Animals Regulations, the LAR, which set out the container requirements by species, stocking density, marking and labelling, documentation, and how animals are accepted, loaded, fed and watered. 2 further layers sit on top and catch people out: the LAR carries state variations, which are the extra rules individual countries impose, and operator variations, which are the extra rules individual airlines impose. A shipment can satisfy the LAR and still be refused because of either. The edition in force on the day you fly is the one that counts, and IATA has published the changes for the 53rd edition, which takes effect for 2027, covering ventilation standards, labelling, cargo compartment cleaning and disinfection, and Pre-Loading Advance Cargo Information.

What paperwork does a live animal shipment need?

A shipper’s certification for live animals, the air waybill, a veterinary health certificate, and the import permit issued by the destination country, which is the document that most often decides the departure date. Protected and endangered species need CITES documents as well, and those are issued by a national management authority rather than by an airline or a broker. The captain is notified that animals are on board. Quarantine arrangements at the far end are agreed before departure, not after landing, because an aircraft cannot wait and animals cannot sit on a ramp.

How are horses flown, and can a groom travel with them?

Horses travel in purpose built stalls that load onto the main deck as units, bedded down with hay and water, and they are walked on and off rather than lifted. How many share a stall depends on the stall design and the size of the horses, and it is set by the stall manufacturer and the regulations rather than by preference. Grooms normally travel on the same aircraft, which the LAR provides for under accompanying persons, and a veterinarian travels with high value or long haul movements. On a charter all of that is arranged as 1 booking rather than as a cargo booking plus a separate set of passenger tickets.

How are cattle, sheep and day old chicks different?

Cattle, sheep, goats and swine travel in pens or containers built to the container requirement for that species, and the number that fits is set by the stocking density in the regulations and by the floor and contour limits of the aircraft, so the honest answer to how many head fit is always an aircraft type and a stall layout rather than a round number. Ventilation is the usual point of failure, in the air and more often on the ground while the aircraft waits. Day old chicks are a different problem entirely: they are a time critical shipment measured in hours from the hatchery, the regulations set how densely they are packed and how they are ventilated, and the schedule decides whether the consignment is worth anything at all.

Can you move zoo animals and exotic species by air?

Yes, and it is usually a charter rather than a freight booking, for 2 reasons that have nothing to do with money. The crate for a large or unusual animal is often too tall for a lower hold and has to go on the main deck, and the welfare clock runs from the moment the animal is boxed rather than from departure, so the flight has to fit the animal’s day instead of the other way round. Keepers, and usually a veterinarian, travel with the animal. What Andreea needs first is the species, the number of animals, the crate dimensions if they exist yet, and whether the species is CITES listed.

What does CITES require for a protected species?

It depends which appendix the species is on. For Appendix I, the most strictly controlled, the movement needs permits from both countries and the import permit is obtained first. It is granted only where the movement is not primarily commercial, will not be detrimental to the survival of the species, and the receiving institution is equipped to house and care for the animal, which means the destination enclosure is part of the paperwork. Appendix II normally needs an export permit from the country of origin. Permits expire: an import permit runs for 1 year and an export permit for 6 months, which is why a delayed flight can mean starting again. CITES also makes humane shipment a condition of issue, and points at the IATA Live Animals Regulations or its own transport guidelines for what that means in practice. Permits come from a national management authority, never from an airline or a broker, and they take months rather than weeks.

Who decides that a zoo animal moves at all?

For animals in a managed breeding programme, the programme does. A European breeding programme issues breeding and transfer recommendations to its member collections to keep genetic diversity across the whole population, and the North American equivalent works through a breeding and transfer plan drawn up for the species. So the receiving collection and the sending collection are usually settled long before anybody asks what it costs to fly. That order is worth knowing, because it means the transport brief arrives with fixed endpoints and often a fixed season, and the job is to find the aircraft that fits them rather than to propose a cheaper routing.

How are the crates built, and what happens before the flight?

The crate is built to the container requirement for that species in the IATA Live Animals Regulations, and for anything unusual it is built specially, measured against the aircraft door and the main deck contour before it is welded rather than after. It needs ventilation on the right faces, spacer bars so the ventilation cannot be blocked by freight or by a net, a way to inspect and feed the animal without opening it, and lifting points that suit a forklift or a crane. The animal is normally conditioned to the crate in the weeks before departure, so that loading day is not the first time it has ever been inside. The crate is also the reason a quote changes: it is bigger than people expect, and it is the crate, not the animal, that decides the aircraft.

What is different about birds, poultry and hatching eggs?

Veterinary import control is the whole game. Avian consignments move under health certification that depends on the disease status of the origin region, and an outbreak can close a corridor at short notice and strand a booking that was compliant the week before, so the route is confirmed against the position on the day rather than against a standing rule. In the aircraft the issues are ventilation, temperature and keeping the hold dark and quiet. Day old chicks and hatching eggs are a different kind of shipment again, closer to a deadline than to a consignment: the value is in arriving quickly and on schedule, and a cheaper aircraft that leaves a day later is not cheaper at all.
